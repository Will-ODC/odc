## New findings

None is `[BLOCKING]`.

### [SHOULD] T3-1: The registrar may make its eligibility decision when it signs, after the seal, so a sealed batch can lose a ballot and strand the issue

- `event-types.md:523–524` (ET-20): eligibility and uniqueness are "checked
  off-log before the registrar signs".
- `event-types.md:844–851` (ET-25): a producer admits a ballot once it passes
  every EV-26 check that does not depend on position. The registrar signs at
  append, after the seal (l.847–849). The paragraph then concludes that a
  sealed batch "never falls short of `ballot_batch_min` … because a ballot in
  it is unappendable".
- Eligibility and uniqueness are not EV-26 checks. So nothing requires the
  registrar's decision to be made at admission, or to be final, and nothing
  obliges the registrar to sign every ballot admitted to a sealed batch.

A registrar that follows ET-20's wording and re-checks at signing can refuse
one ballot of a sealed batch. Examples: the same voter admitted to two batches,
or eligibility revoked between admission and append. When that batch was
sealed at exactly `ballot_batch_min`, what can be appended is under-size. That
is `VALID` at the tail, so EV-26 allows it. Every later batch of the issue then
ends it, and EV-26 forbids appending any of them. Every ballot already admitted
to the issue's open batch is stranded silently, and the under-size rule at
l.861–865 has not fired, because the producer never sealed an under-size
batch. It also breaks "appended in full" (l.842–843).

**Demonstrated:** `registrar-refuses-after-seal.ndjson` (the two signed
ballots) is `VALID`. `registrar-refuses-then-next-batch.ndjson`, the next full
batch, is `INVALID at line 5` on both verifiers.

**Fix (additive, producer-only), in ET-25:**

> The registrar's eligibility and uniqueness decision (ET-20) is made before
> admission and is final. The registrar MUST sign every ballot admitted to a
> sealed batch, and refuses a ballot only before admission.

In ET-20, change "before the registrar signs" to "before the ballot is
admitted (ET-25)". Land this before identity's first merge and the Phase 1
write path. It does not gate RC: it changes no verdict and is additive.

### [SHOULD] T3-2: ET-21a binds only the ledger and the registrar, but other operator-run components sit on the submission path

ET-21a (`event-types.md:576–586`) names "the ledger nor the registrar". Charter
§9 makes the MCP server the canonical interface, and `services/web` and
`services/mcp` are operator-run clients that will relay ballots. Item 2 bars
only statements "a third party could verify came from the ledger or the
registrar". A confirmation sent by the operator's web or MCP service and signed
by the operator's domain, for example a DKIM-signed email saying "your vote for
choice 1 on issue X was recorded", falls outside the letter of the rule. That
is a transferable receipt of a ballot's contents (charter §5, §8).

Item 1 has the same gap. Under the one-open-batch rule, any relaying component
knows which batch holds the ballot: the next batch of that issue to be
appended.

Third-party front-ends cannot be bound by `contracts/`, and ET-21a's closing
residual covers them correctly. The operator's own components can be bound.

**Fix (additive):** extend ET-21a's subject to "the ledger, the registrar, or
any other component the operator runs on the submission path". This gates
Phase 2 (`web`) and Phase 3 (`mcp`), not RC.

### [NIT] T3-3: ET-25 overstates what EV-26 forbids after an under-size batch

`event-types.md:865–868` says that once an under-size batch is appended, "EV-26
forbids appending any later ballot of that issue". A later ballot at the same
instant, with no other ballot of the issue between, extends the batch, and the
chain stays `VALID`. What forbids it is ET-25's own seal ("no ballot admitted
after the seal"), not EV-26.

**Demonstrated:** `undersize-tail-extended-same-instant.ndjson` is `VALID` on
both verifiers. Say "any later ballot of that issue at another instant".

### [NIT] T3-4: Nothing obliges a producer to ever seal or append, and the close of an issue is invisible

No rule requires an open batch ever to be sealed, or a sealed batch to be
appended. Meanwhile ET-21a lets the producer tell the voter "accepted". A
conforming producer can therefore hold admitted ballots forever, and the log
cannot show it. ET-20 already accepts that a malicious registrar may drop
ballots, and ET-25 (l.870–873) states that the decision to close an issue is
invisible.

The second re-audit's question about a visible `issue_closed` event
(additive) does not appear in the `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` sections I may read.
Consider filing it.

## The questions

**1. Does any spec field leak identity into the public log?** No.

- `vote_cast` is `{issue_id, choice, sig}` (l.478–482).
- `participant_registered` holds only a pseudonymous `pubkey`.
- The only free text is `issue_created.title` and `genesis.contracts`, and the
  operator writes both.
- #417 added no field. The linkage map appears nowhere in `contracts/`.

The channels outside the fields are unchanged and are now all stated in the
spec text:

- the registrar's 64 chosen signature bytes (l.551–553);
- registration-to-ballot adjacency (l.553–556);
- turnout arithmetic (ET-14b note);
- the registrar's view of final lines (l.532–535, S-C).

**2. Is receipt-freeness compromised by any spec artifact?** No.

- The voter holds no key and gets no field.
- ET-21a now bars the ledger and registrar from singling out a line or batch,
  and from any third-party-verifiable statement.
- `marker-64.ndjson` is still `VALID`. That is the bounded marker channel
  charter §5 leaves open.
- The remaining gaps are T3-2 (operator components not named) and S-C (the
  registrar, which is trusted by policy).

**3. Can the operator equivocate within the spec as written?** Not with two
chains: they have different genesis hashes, and `--chain` rejects the wrong one
(re-run). A common-prefix fork is caught only against an anchored head, and the
anchor is unspecified (S-D). Within one chain, the operator still decides
alone, and invisibly, when an issue closes (T3-4).

**Agent checklist:**

- **Ballot and identity join:** none in `contracts/`. The registrar's
  position view is S-C.
- **Linkage-map fields in errors, logs or metrics:** none defined. RA-11 has
  fixed codes, and EX-24 reports stored hashes only.
- **Voter-constructible proof:** none on the log. On the write path, ET-21a
  covers the ledger and registrar (scope gap T3-2).
- **Mutating or deleting event rows:** EV-1, EV-13, RA-13 and EV-26. No
  storage, ORM, migration or superuser code exists yet to audit.
- **Ballot and sentiment sharing a store, queue or endpoint:** barred
  permanently (EV-22). EV-26 forbids appending unregistered types.
- **Signatures verified before append:** a contract obligation (EV-26).

**Do the verifiers' verdicts follow from `contracts/` alone?** Yes, on
everything I checked.

- The first re-audit's spec-only encoder reproduces the `hashing.md` §6 digest
  `78ed980b…` and its signature, and is byte-identical to vector 001.
- The fixtures regenerate byte-identically: 150 vectors, 156 files.
- `MANIFEST.sha256` verifies: 154 entries.

**Do the two verifiers agree on all 150 vectors?** Yes, on verdict and line
set: 23 `VALID`, 15 `PARTIAL`, 112 `INVALID`, with 0 mismatches against
`index.json`.

**Do the rules added in #410 and #417 contradict, strand, deadlock, give a
receipt, or make a producer impossible?**

- **Contradiction:** none between EV-26, ET-21a, the seal, the under-size rule
  and the three one-open-batch rules. ET-24a's "used instant" counts appended
  ballots and rule 2 counts sealed ones too, so rule 2 is the stricter of the
  two. Rule 3 forbids interleaving only within one issue, which matches ET-24.
  `two-issues-interleaved-runs.ndjson` (two issues' runs interleaved, with A's
  last batch under-size) is `VALID` on both verifiers.
- **Stranding:** closed for the cases NS-1 named, with the one remaining route
  in T3-1.
- **Deadlock:** none. Each issue's queue is independent. A stalled registrar
  stalls only the issues whose sealed batches await signatures.
  Registrations interleave freely (ET-24).
- **Receipt:** none from the rules themselves. The "accepted" answer ET-21a
  allows gives the submitter nothing a third party can check. Its timing could
  single out the batch, but under rule 1 the time of the vote already does that
  for any timing observer (ET-21 l.560–562).
- **Impossible producer:** no. A ledger that holds the tail, has the registrar
  sign each ballot at its fixed line, verifies (EV-26) and appends satisfies
  every rule. The cost is NS-2's liveness trade-off, already filed.

## What is sound

- **The #417 stranding argument (l.886–891) is correct under its
  assumptions:** one open batch, unused instants, seal-order runs, and an
  under-size seal that ends admission. Only ET-20's timing of the eligibility
  decision falls outside it (T3-1).
- **ET-21a is the right shape.** It separates marking a line (item 1) from a
  transferable statement (item 2). It allows an unauthenticated
  "accepted/refused" answer and the public log. It names the residual a remote
  ballot cannot remove. It is placed outside both verification stages.
- **The ET-21 text now states the registrar's view honestly**, so S-C is a
  design question and no longer a wording gap.
- **Position binding in HA-15 still defeats replay**
  (`ballot-replayed.ndjson`: `INVALID at line 6` on both).
- **The corpus, fixtures and rehearsal remain reproducible from the prose.**

## Design questions for filing (not filed)

My brief allows me to write only this report and its generator folder, so I
did not edit `memory/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`.

1. **From T3-1:** at which step does the registrar decide eligibility and
   uniqueness, and must it sign every admitted ballot? This interacts with S-C:
   keeping admission apart from signing must not move the eligibility decision
   to signing.
2. **From T3-4, and the second re-audit's NS-1 suggestion:** should a visible
   `issue_closed` event, which would be additive, record when the operator ends
   admission, so that readers can see it and withheld ballots become
   detectable?
