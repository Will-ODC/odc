## New findings

None is `[BLOCKING]`.

### [SHOULD] NS-1: A sealed batch can become unappendable, so admitted ballots are stranded with no rule saying what happens to them

- `event-types.md:641–643` (ET-24) lets an issue's **last** batch be
  under-size.
- `evolution.md:476–485` (EV-26) lets a ledger append whatever leaves the
  export `VALID`. An under-size batch at an issue's tail is `VALID`, so EV-26
  permits it.
- Once it is appended, every later batch of that issue would end it, and the
  export would be `INVALID`, so EV-26 forbids appending any of them.
- But ET-25 (`event-types.md:812–815`) says a sealed batch "is then appended in
  full: every ballot admitted to it".

Nothing in ET-25 stops a producer from sealing a batch below
`ballot_batch_min` without meaning it to be the last. Nothing says that a
producer which has published an under-size batch must stop admitting ballots
to that issue. So a conforming producer can admit ballots (and the registrar
records those voters as having voted, ET-20) that no rule lets it ever append.
Those voters are disenfranchised in silence, and the log cannot show it.

**Demonstrated:** `undersize-tail.ndjson` (two ballots, minimum 3) is `VALID`.
`undersize-then-next-batch.ndjson`, the next sealed batch of three, is
`INVALID at line 5` on both verifiers.

There is no `issue_closed` event, so the decision that a batch is the last one
is also the operator's alone, and readers never see it. That last point is
already a residual in ET-21 (l.564–565).

**Fix (additive, producer-only):** add to ET-25:

> A producer MUST NOT seal a batch smaller than `ballot_batch_min` unless it
> admits no further ballot to that issue. Once such a batch is appended, the
> producer MUST refuse admission to that issue before any eligibility record
> is made.

Land it before the Phase 1 write path.

### [SHOULD] NS-2: A registrant's self-signature covers its final `seq`, `ts` and `prev_hash`, so registration is an interactive step inside the append path

- `hashing.md:134–139` (HA-15) builds every signing preimage over `seq`, `ts`
  and `prev_hash`.
- `event-types.md:393–395` (ET-10) makes `participant_registered` signed by the
  registrant, a member of the public.
- EV-26 now makes the ledger refuse any event whose signature fails at its
  final position.

Together, a registrant can sign only after the ledger has fixed the exact tail
the event will extend. Any append in between, including the sequence of
registrar-signed ballots of a sealed batch, invalidates the signature.

**Demonstrated:** `registration-moved-one-line.ndjson`. A registration signed
for line 3 and appended at line 4, because another registration went first, is
`INVALID at line 4` on both verifiers.

A Phase 1 ledger therefore has two choices:

- hold a tail reservation open across a client round-trip, which any slow or
  hostile registrant can use to stall the whole log, ballots included;
- or reject and retry, which livelocks under load.

`contracts/` is silent on both, and RA-13 (`read-api.md:88–90`) puts the write
interface outside `contracts/`.

The binding is not useless. It is what makes a captured registration (or
ballot) non-replayable at another line: see `ballot-replayed.ndjson` under
S-C. So the fix is a design choice, not a deletion.

- **Option 1:** keep the binding and specify a reservation protocol with a
  timeout in the ledger.
- **Option 2:** for registrations, sign a position-free statement and accept
  duplicate registrations. `pubkey-registered-twice.ndjson` is already
  `VALID` under ET-12, so derived views must deduplicate either way.

Option 2 changes `hashing.md` or adds a new `participant_registered` version.
The `hashing.md` change is possible only before the freeze. **Decide this with
S-C, before the ledger write path is coded and before the freeze.** I have
written it up as a design question below; I did not file it.

### [SHOULD] NS-3: No contract rule bars the write path from giving a voter a receipt

`event-types.md:527–531` (ET-21) rests receipt-freeness on "nothing a voter
retains that binds them to a log line". But `read-api.md:88–90` (RA-13, v2)
now says explicitly that what the submitter is told is outside `contracts/`.

A write path that returns a ballot's `seq`, `hash` or `sig` gives the voter
nothing provable. Any voter could point at any line with the coerced choice.
An _authenticated_ acknowledgement is a different matter: one signed by the
registrar or the ledger, binding the submission to a line. That is a
transferable receipt, and the charter forbids it (§5 "receipt-free by design",
§8 non-negotiable).

ET-25's seal makes a synchronous answer impossible, but not a later
notification. This is the agent checklist's third question, and today the
answer depends on Phase 1 code that does not exist yet.

**Fix (additive, producer-only, in the register of ET-25):**

> Neither the ledger nor the registrar MAY give a ballot's submitter, or
> anyone acting for them, any value that identifies the ballot's line
> (`seq`, `hash`, `sig`, `prev_hash` or batch instant), or any authenticated
> statement that the submission was appended.

Land it before identity's first merge.

### [NIT] NS-4: ET-21 and ET-25 now describe the registrar's view differently

ET-25 (`event-types.md:819–821`) puts the registrar at the append.
ET-21 (`event-types.md:569–571`) says it sees each ballot "at admission". Make
ET-21 say that it also sees each ballot's final line. This is the S-C wording,
and it can land now.

### [NIT] NS-5: ET-25 says "a later batch instant"

`event-types.md:823–825` reads as an order on `ts`, which ES-21 never permits
(`event-schema.md:127–142`). ET-24a needs only an instant the issue has not
used. Say "an instant that issue has not used (ET-24a)".

`far-future-instant.ndjson` (ballots at `9999-12-31T23:59`) is `VALID` on both
verifiers. A batch instant need not be near real time, which is fine, but it
shows that "later" carries no checked meaning.

## The questions

**1. Does any spec field leak identity into the public log?** No.

- `vote_cast` is `{issue_id, choice, sig}` (l.478–482).
- `participant_registered` holds only a pseudonymous `pubkey`.
- The only free text is `issue_created.title` and `genesis.contracts`, and the
  operator writes both.
- EV-26 and the new ET-25 add no field.
- The linkage map appears nowhere in `contracts/`.

The channels outside the fields are unchanged and stated:

- the registrar's 64 chosen signature bytes (ET-21 l.549–551);
- registration-to-ballot adjacency (l.552–554);
- turnout arithmetic (ET-14b note);
- the registrar's view of final positions (S-C);
- write-path responses (NS-3).

**2. Is receipt-freeness compromised by any spec artifact?** Not by any
artifact the log carries.

- The voter holds no key and gets no field.
- A sealed batch hides the line among at least `ballot_batch_min` ballots
  against a timing observer.
- ET-21 now says honestly that the choice is only as hidden as the batch tally
  and that a final batch may hold one ballot.

`marker-64.ndjson` is still `VALID`, the bounded marker channel charter §5
leaves open. The gap is outside the log, in what the write path may tell a
voter (NS-3).

**3. Can the operator equivocate within the spec as written?** Two chains are
distinguishable by genesis hash (F1, re-demonstrated). A common-prefix fork is
caught only against an anchored head, and the anchor is unspecified (S-D).
Within one chain, the operator still decides alone, and invisibly, when an
issue's last batch is (NS-1, ET-21).

**Agent checklist:**

- **Ballot and identity join:** none in `contracts/`. The registrar's
  position view is S-C.
- **Linkage-map fields in errors, logs or metrics:** none defined. RA-11 has
  two fixed codes, and EX-24 reports stored hashes only.
- **Voter-constructible proof:** none on the log. Write path: NS-3.
- **Mutating or deleting events:** EV-1, EV-13, RA-13. EV-26 now forbids even
  an append to a non-`VALID` chain. No storage, ORM or migration code exists to
  audit.
- **Ballot and sentiment sharing a store:** barred permanently (EV-22). EV-26
  forbids appending any unregistered type.
- **Signatures verified before append:** now a contract obligation (EV-26).
  NS-2 is the consequence for the write path.

**Do the verifiers' verdicts follow from `contracts/` alone?** Yes, on
everything I checked.

- The first re-audit's spec-only encoder still reproduces the §6 digest and
  signature and is byte-identical to vector 001 (`selfcheck.mjs`).
- Fixtures regenerate byte-identically from `tools/fixtures-gen`.
- Both verifiers match `index.json` on 150/150.
- Neither verifier's non-test source reads `contracts/fixtures/`, the
  environment or the network. The only 64-hex literal in either is the 64-zero
  anchor (grep).

**Do the two verifiers agree on the full corpus?** Yes, 150/150 on verdict and
line set: 23 `VALID`, 15 `PARTIAL`, 112 `INVALID`.

**Do EV-26, ET-25 and ET-21 introduce a contradiction or a new hole?**

- One tension: EV-26's "never append unless `VALID`" against ET-25's "appended
  in full" after an under-size seal (NS-1).
- One consequence made sharper: interactive registration (NS-2).
- One wording drift: ET-21 against ET-25 on the registrar (NS-4).
- EV-15 was updated to place EV-26 outside the stage split (l.210–211), and
  RA-13 points at EV-26 consistently.
- No verdict changes. No vector depends on EV-26 or ET-25.

## What is sound

- **EV-26 is the right rule, stated as a result rather than a method.** It
  forbids appends to a non-`VALID` chain, including after a version rollback.
  It allows incremental checking. It is honestly marked unverifiable from the
  log.
- **ET-25's seal closes the drip-feed loophole** without any wire change. ET-21
  now promises exactly the k-bound (`ballot_batch_min`) and no more, including
  the tally and last-batch caveats.
- **Position binding in HA-15 defeats replay** of both registrations and
  ballots (`ballot-replayed`, `registration-moved-one-line`). Whatever S-C and
  NS-2 decide must keep that property.
- **Vectors 146–150** pin HA-2's ill-formed-UTF-8 rule, and both verifiers
  reject at the right line.
- The corpus, the rehearsal and the fixtures remain reproducible from the prose.
